Endpoint notation

The entity-box layout, named relationships and ISA are preserved. Cardinality symbols appear at the entity being counted: circle plus bar for zero-or-one; two bars for exactly-one; circle plus crow’s foot for zero-or-many; bar plus crow’s foot for one-or-many. The last symbol includes its mandatory bar explicitly.

The same 26-entity, 42-relationship business model is retained. This is the requested presentation style, combining the earlier named relationship diamonds with crow’s-foot endpoint symbols.

Business model

The model separates a legal provider from its outlets, local customer accounts from verified identity assertions, event evaluations from periodic evaluations, and exact rule versions from stable award entitlement identities.

The original journey is supported: Dealer A sells VIN1; Provider C services VIN1; Provider B rents replacement VIN2. The rental points to the service. Verified account-link evidence connects the relevant customer records without inventing a global customer ID. Cross-provider contributions do not automatically change the award beneficiary.

VehicleActivity ISA is total and disjoint: every activity is exactly one Sale, Rental or Service. The complete natural activity identity is inherited by each subtype.

Keys are proposed existing business identities, not generated IDs. Customer-account references, outlet references, source namespaces and published program/rule/metric vocabularies must be verified against actual records. A code-like label alone does not prove that a natural key exists.

The agreed core retains one final result per evaluation/version/scope and measurement grain. Detailed rerun, source-revision, identity re-verification, financial reversal and benefit-redemption histories require separate business identities. Immutable decision evidence is mandatory for finalized assessments.

The downloadable specification and consensus report define cardinalities, effective-date selection, proof paths, benefit units and the acceptance scenarios. This review approves a bounded conceptual model; it does not prove a production database implementation.
